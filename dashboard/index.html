    How it works

    Data: the open F1DB dataset, updated after every race.

    Each driver gets inputs known before the weekend starts: recent finishes and qualifying positions, points per race, the team's qualifying pace, retirements, head-to-head against the team-mate, past results at the circuit, and how much this circuit rewards pole.

    Two models score every driver: a regularised logistic regression and a small LightGBM tree model. Their average is rescaled so the field's win chances add up to one. Separate models of the same design predict pole and podium. Older seasons count for less, because regulations change.

    The test: every race since the start of the 2022 season was predicted by a model retrained on only the races before it. The baseline gives each driver a chance in proportion to their recent points.

    These are pre-qualifying odds. Grid position is the strongest single signal in F1, so they are most useful before Saturday. Later rounds use form as of the latest race and will shift as results come in.
